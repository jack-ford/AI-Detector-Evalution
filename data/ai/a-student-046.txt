I am writing in favor of requiring all students to do some community service before they graduate. Plenty of my classmates will say their schedules are already packed, which is a reasonable worry. Even so, I believe what students gain is worth the time it costs.

For one thing, service ties students to the place they live. Most of us shuttle between home, school and practice and never think about who keeps the park tidy or who stocks the food pantry on Elm Street. A few Saturdays doing the work ourselves would change how we look at our town. Last spring I spent one afternoon sorting donations at the pantry, and in the line I was startled to see two families from my own neighborhood.

For another, service builds skills that classes don't. Arriving on time for people who depend on you, taking instructions from an adult who isn't a teacher, and doing a boring job properly are all things employers look for.

The requirement needn't be burdensome. Ten hours a semester, chosen from a list of approved places, would get students started without costing them grades or sports, and many would carry on by choice.

I hope you will think about making service part of what graduating from our school means.
