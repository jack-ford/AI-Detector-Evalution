The big Scot let the rifle drop to the crook of his arm and stood in the doorway, staring. He had come up the frozen creek ready for trouble, and the want of it left him oddly sour. But the room took the sourness out of him in a hurry.

"Man, will you look at it," he said. "Clocks. Forty of them if there's one, and every last one stopped dead. Brass ones, and wee painted ones, and yon great coffin of a thing in the corner. Who in creation packs clocks six hundred miles north of the steel?"

Carrick did not answer. He had crossed to the bench under the window and was turning over a litter of fine tools, files no thicker than a needle, a jeweller's glass, a tray of tiny wheels furred with dust. The stove was cold. Whoever had sat at that bench had been a craftsman, and patient, and he had been gone a long while.
