Pruitt's Moon dropped away under them, brown and then tan and then the color of weak tea, and Harv kept both hands on the yoke because the left stabilizer still shuddered whenever he let go.

Nobody talked. Lila was peeling a ration bar and not eating it. Dunc had his boots up on the chart locker, which he had been told about.

The galley hatch banged. Aunt Maudie came through it backward, dragging a crate, with a look on her face like she had just won at cards.

"We up?"

"We're up," Harv said. "Forty kilometers. Don't ask me how long the stabilizer holds."

"It'll hold. I packed it with lard." She sat down on the crate and fanned herself. "Eleven days you boys were under that engine with your meters. And what does it want? Grease. Kitchen grease, same as a hinge."

Dunc took his boots down. "You put lard in a Kessler coupling."

"Six pounds. The rest went to the mayor's wife, and she gave me this." Maudie thumped the crate. "Forty jars of whatever they pickle down there. She says a jar buys a goat. I didn't ask what a goat buys."

Lila finally bit the ration bar. "They'll charge us import duty on that at Ceres."

"On pickles?"

"On anything in glass."

Maudie considered this for the length of time it took the stabilizer to shudder twice. Then she got up and started moving jars into the laundry.
