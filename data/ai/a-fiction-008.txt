"You think it a small thing, then," said he, turning from the window, "to be sent after salt."

I confessed that I did. I had hoped for Jamaica, or the Guinea coast at the least.

"So thought I at your age. Sit down, Tobias, and I will mend your opinion. What feeds the west country through the winter?"

"Fish, sir."

"Fish. And what keeps a cod from rotting between the Banks and Bristol quay? You may have ten thousand quintals in your hold, and without salt you have ten thousand quintals of stink. Now, where does our salt come from? It comes from the pans at Setubal and from the Bay of Bourgneuf, which is to say from the King of Portugal and the King of France. Do you begin to see?"

"I see that we buy from our enemies."

"We buy from one enemy and one uncertain friend. And mark what follows. The fishery is the nursery of seamen. Every lad who hauls a line off Newfoundland is a hand the Navy may press when the next war comes, and it will come before you are thirty. Starve the fishery of salt and you starve the fleet of men. The French know it. Their agents have been in Lisbon since Michaelmas with more gold than I can match, bidding for the whole of next summer's rake."

He let that sink in while he filled his pipe. I had known Mr. Penhallow six years, first as my father's creditor and afterward as my own master, and had never heard him speak so many words together on any matter but freight charges.

"Then I am to outbid them," I said.

"You are to do nothing so foolish. You have not the money. You are to find the man who sold them his promise and learn what would persuade him to break it."
