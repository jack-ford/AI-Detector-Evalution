What gives KFC-style chicken its rugged, shattering crust is mainly how you treat the flour, not any hidden ingredient.

First, thin out your flour with cornstarch, about three parts flour to one part cornstarch, and season generously with salt, pepper, paprika and garlic powder. Because cornstarch has no gluten, it fries up crisp instead of doughy. Adding a teaspoon of baking powder for every cup of flour helps too: it makes small bubbles that keep the coating light.

Second, take two or three tablespoons of whatever wet mixture you're using (buttermilk, or an egg beaten into milk), drizzle them over the seasoned flour, and work them in with your fingers until the flour forms small, ragged lumps. Those lumps cling to the chicken and turn into the craggy, crunchy bits.

Then let the chicken sit in the buttermilk, press each piece hard into the flour, and put the coated pieces on a rack to rest for 15 to 20 minutes before they go in the oil. Resting lets the coating absorb moisture, so it stays put while frying.

Keep the oil near 325°F (165°C) and avoid crowding the pot; a sharp drop in temperature leaves the breading greasy and soft. Let the pieces drain on a wire rack, since paper towels trap steam and soften the underside. If you want extra crunch, dip twice: flour, buttermilk, flour again.
