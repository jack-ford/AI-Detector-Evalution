Hallam had stopped counting the hours somewhere past the second watch change. The drive hummed under his boots, steady enough. He kept his eyes on the plot. The patrol ship was still there, a hard white point eleven thousand miles astern, and it had not gained or lost a mile since the Corvane cleared the belt.

That was the part he could not get past. A hunter that closed the distance could be fought or dodged. This one simply waited, matching each change of course with a patience that seemed to belong to something other than men. It had followed them through the debris fields off Tarsis, where Odell had taken the ship between tumbling rocks at a speed that made the hull plates sing. It had followed them around the gas giant and into the storm of charged particles in its shadow, where every instrument aboard went blind for the better part of an hour. Each time they had come out the far side believing themselves alone, and each time the white point had crept back onto the screen.

He thought of Varn sitting on that other bridge with his hands folded, not hurrying. Varn had never hurried. At the Academy he had won at chess the same way, giving up nothing and letting the other man wear himself out.

"Fuel state," Odell said, without turning her head.

The engineer read off the figures in a flat voice. Nobody commented on them. Old Dr. Pryce sat on a locker by the hatch with the sample case across his knees, as he had sat for two days, and his eyes moved from one face to another.

Odell rubbed her jaw. She looked at the plot for a long moment, then at the chart of the system ahead, with its single dead moon.

"We can't land," she said. "Not there, and not with him watching. The minute we cut the drive he'll be on top of us, and he knows it as well as I do."
