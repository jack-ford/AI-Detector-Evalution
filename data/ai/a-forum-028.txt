Your legs are pendulums. A leg of a given length wants to swing at a particular rate, and when you walk at your own pace most of the swing is free: gravity brings the leg forward and you only top up what was lost. My natural rate is about 115 steps a minute. My father-in-law's is closer to 95.

Walk beside him and I have two choices. I can keep my cadence and chop my stride down to something silly, or keep my stride and slow each step. Both cost muscle. Slowing the swing means the hip flexors have to brake the leg and then hold it, and the calf no longer gets its cheap rebound off the Achilles tendon because the foot sits on the ground too long for the stretch to be returned. You end up lowering yourself onto each step instead of rolling over it.

There's a balance cost too. At 3 mph you spend very little time on one foot. At 1.5 you are standing on one leg for a good part of every stride, and the small muscles around the ankle and hip work the whole time to keep you upright. That is why an hour of museum shuffling hurts more than an hour on a trail.
