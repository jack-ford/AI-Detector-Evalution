"Sleep in the haymow? Well, I d'know. You ain't got matches on ye, have ye? Turn out them pockets."

Ben turned his out. A jackknife, a wad of fish line, half a cracker. The old man poked at the cracker with one brown finger and grunted.

"That all the supper you fetched?"

"We ate the rest at noon," said Ben. Joey, behind him, said nothing at all; he was looking at the pie cooling on the window ledge.

"Huh. Mother!" The farmer leaned back through the door and shouted it. "Mother, set two more plates. There's a couple o' tramps out here, 'bout four foot high." He held the door open with his boot. "Wipe your feet. She's partic'lar. And don't you set in the rocker with the red cushion, that's the cat's."
