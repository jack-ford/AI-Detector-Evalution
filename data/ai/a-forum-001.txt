The craggy, shattering crust you get from KFC-style chicken comes mostly from how the flour is handled rather than from a secret ingredient.

Start by cutting your flour with cornstarch, roughly three parts flour to one part cornstarch, and season it heavily with salt, pepper, paprika and garlic powder. Cornstarch contains no gluten, so it fries up brittle rather than bready. A teaspoon of baking powder per cup of flour also helps, since it creates tiny bubbles that lighten the coating.

Next, drizzle two or three tablespoons of your wet mixture (buttermilk, or egg beaten with milk) into the seasoned flour and rub it in with your fingertips until you have small, shaggy clumps. Those clumps stick to the chicken and become the rough, crunchy bits.

Soak the chicken in the buttermilk, press it firmly into the flour, then let the coated pieces rest on a rack for 15 to 20 minutes before frying. The rest lets the coating hydrate so it doesn't fall off in the oil.

Fry at about 325°F (165°C), and don't crowd the pot, because a big temperature drop gives you greasy, soft breading. Drain the pieces on a wire rack instead of paper towels so steam doesn't soften the underside. For even more crunch, double-dip: flour, buttermilk, then flour again.
