Mostly it's about heat. Whole butter is roughly 80 percent fat, with the rest being water and milk proteins. Those proteins start to scorch at around 150°C, which is why butter goes brown and then bitter in a hot pan. Skim them off, cook out the water, and what's left is nearly pure fat that tolerates searing temperatures closer to 230°C without burning.

There are side benefits. With no water, it doesn't spit, and it keeps far longer, since the solids are what spoil first. That's the logic behind ghee. It also stays clear when melted, which matters for things like hollandaise or for dipping lobster, where cloudy butter looks and tastes muddier.
