For most of the past century, going to school has meant sitting in a classroom with a teacher at the front of the room. Technology has changed that. Today a student with a laptop and an internet connection can watch lessons, turn in assignments, and talk with teachers without ever leaving home. Some people worry that this kind of learning is a poor substitute for the real thing, but I believe schools should offer distance learning as an option. Attending class from home gives students a flexible schedule, helps those who cannot easily get to school, and teaches independence.

The most obvious benefit of distance learning is flexibility. In a traditional school, every student follows the same bell schedule whether or not it suits them. Online classes let students work at the times when they focus best. A student who understands a math lesson quickly can move ahead, while one who is struggling can replay the video as many times as necessary without feeling embarrassed in front of classmates. Students with jobs, sports, or family responsibilities can also arrange their schoolwork around those commitments instead of falling behind.

Distance learning also helps students who have trouble getting to school in the first place. A student recovering from surgery or living with a chronic illness might miss weeks of class.
