I spent my first fourteen months out of school fixing bugs in a billing system written in 2003. Hated it for about six of them. Then one afternoon I traced a rounding error through four stored procedures and a cron job nobody remembered, and realised I could now read other people's ugly code faster than anyone who'd joined with me.

That's what you're being paid to learn right now, whether or not anyone told you so at the interview. New features are the easy part. Working out why a function someone wrote eight years ago returns null on Tuesdays is harder, and it's most of the job at most companies.

Whether to stay is your call and I'm not going to make it. But nothing stops you sending out CVs on a Sunday while the paycheques keep arriving.
