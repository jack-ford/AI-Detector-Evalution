The old Dutchman had just set the kettle back on the stove when a car stopped in the lane outside. He stood perfectly still, one hand on the kettle's handle. Then he moved, faster than Ridley would have believed possible, sweeping the two extra cups into the bread box and kicking the rug flat over the trapdoor. He pressed a finger to his lips, straightened his jacket, and went out through the shop, pulling the kitchen door shut after him.

Ridley did not look at Mac. He did not need to. The air in the little room had gone thick and still, and his pulse hammered in his ears so loudly he was sure it could be heard in the street.

Were they about to be handed over? The thought went through him like a cold blade.
