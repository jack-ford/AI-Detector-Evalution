"That's not the point, though. He didn't take the money, and half the form knows it, and still nobody will sit with him at table. I call that cowardly."

"It isn't cowardice exactly, Margaret. Fellows just go along with the crowd."

"Then somebody ought to stop going along." She put down the racket she had been restringing and looked at him squarely. "You're captain of the eleven, Ned. If you walked across the dining hall to-night and took the chair beside Lawrence, there would be six others at that table before the soup was cold."

"You make it sound mighty simple."

"It is simple. It's only uncomfortable, and that's a different thing."

Bradley laughed, rather shortly, and dug at the turf with his heel. "Suppose I do it, and Tom Wheelock's crowd cuts me too?"

"Then you'll have found out what they're worth. Will you?"

"I'll think about it."

"That means yes," said Margaret, and picked up her racket again.

He walked back to the dormitory and told Hollis the whole of it.

"She's right about Wheelock, anyhow," said Hollis.
