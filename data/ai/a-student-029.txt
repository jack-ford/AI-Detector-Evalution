You think aliens built it. I get why. The 1976 Viking photo really does look like a face, with eyes and a mouth staring up out of Cydonia, and I'd probably have believed it too if that blurry picture was all we had.

It isn't all we have. Each dot in the Viking photo covered about 43 meters of ground. That's like trying to recognize your friend from a photo made of nine squares. In April 1998 Mars Global Surveyor flew over the same spot with a camera ten times sharper, and the face was gone. What showed up was a lumpy hill.

People said it was hazy that day, that the alien markings were hidden by clouds. Fine. So NASA went back on April 8, 2001, a clear summer day in Cydonia, and shot it again at 1.56 meters per pixel. At that sharpness you could spot a garden shed if one were sitting there. No pyramids. No roads leading up to it.

What the picture does look like is a butte. We have them all over Idaho and Arizona, flat-topped piles of rock left behind after everything softer around them wore away. The eyes and mouth in the old photo were shadows, and shadows move when the sun does. Ask yourself why the face only shows up in the one picture where you can barely see anything.
