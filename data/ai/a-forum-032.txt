The boy has watched one case up close and drawn a conclusion from it. Fair enough, he's a kid.

So give him more cases. Does he know what his father earned in the years he was working, and what the men on the street who left school at fifteen earned in those years? Put the figures on the table. A degree did not fail his father for twenty years; a layoff failed him for the last two. A fourteen-year-old can't see that far back.

I'd also stop making the father deliver the lecture. He is the worst-placed person to argue that school pays, and the son knows it. An uncle, a neighbour, the old colleague who got rehired in March: let one of them say it.

What the father can do is be seen looking. Every morning, kitchen table, applications out, phone calls made where the boy can hear them. My own father was out of work for fourteen months when I was twelve, and that is what I remember.
