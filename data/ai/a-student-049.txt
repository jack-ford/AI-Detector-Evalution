At our school almost every student has a cell phone, and no policy will change that. What matters is what we may do with them after we walk in the door. Of the two policies under discussion, I think the better choice is to allow phones at school and let students use them at lunch and during other free periods, provided they are off during class.

Safety and plain logistics are the best reason. Practices are canceled, buses arrive late, and parents' work plans change halfway through the day. A student who can text quickly at lunch fixes all of that in thirty seconds. With a complete ban, that same student has to request a pass, wait in line for the office phone, and hope a parent will answer a call from a number they don't know. The office staff already have plenty to do without serving as a message service for nine hundred students.

A total ban would also be hard to enforce. Teachers would spend class time hunting for phones tucked into pockets and backpacks rather than teaching. Students tend to follow a rule they find reasonable, and restricting phones to free time is such a rule.
