Heat, mostly. Whole butter is about 80 percent fat; the remainder is water and milk proteins. Around 150°C those proteins begin to scorch, which is the reason butter browns and then turns bitter in a hot pan. Remove them and boil off the water, and the nearly pure fat that's left can take searing temperatures of around 230°C with no burning.

Other benefits come along with it. Without water, the fat doesn't spatter, and it keeps far longer, because the solids spoil first. That's the idea behind ghee. When melted it also stays clear, which matters for hollandaise or for dunking lobster, where cloudy butter looks and tastes muddier.
