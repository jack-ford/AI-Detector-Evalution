No sooner had the old Dutchman returned the kettle to the stove than a car pulled up in the lane. He froze with a hand still on the handle. Next instant he was in motion, quicker than Ridley would have thought him capable of, dropping the two spare cups into the bread box and stamping the rug flat across the trapdoor. A finger went to his lips. He smoothed his jacket, passed through the shop, and drew the kitchen door closed behind him.

Ridley avoided looking at Mac, and had no need to look. In the small room the air had turned heavy and still, and the blood thudded in his ears so loudly that surely the street could hear it.

Were they about to be turned in? Cold as a blade, the thought ran through him.
