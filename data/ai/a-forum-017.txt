You can start as early as you like, including the first weeks. A newborn gets nothing from the plot, but the benefits at that age don't depend on the plot.

The main one is language exposure. Babies learn speech sounds and rhythm from hearing a lot of speech directed at them, and books make adults say more words, and more varied words, than they use while changing diapers or making dinner. Studies of early vocabulary consistently find that the amount of talk a child hears in the first years predicts later language skills, and reading aloud is an easy way to add to it.

Later, around six months, books become objects of interest in themselves. The baby looks at pictures, grabs and chews the pages, and begins to connect your words with what's on the page ("where's the dog?"). By nine to twelve months many babies point, turn pages and bring you a favorite book, which is the start of shared attention and of treating reading as something enjoyable.

There's also the plain fact that it's time spent close to you, hearing your voice, in a calm routine. That is good for a baby regardless of what's being read; in the early months the newspaper works about as well as a picture book.
