The "Face on Mars" is a natural landform, not a monument left behind by an alien civilization. When the Viking 1 spacecraft photographed the Cydonia region in 1976, one of the images showed a mesa that looked remarkably like a human face, complete with eyes, a nose, and a mouth. The picture quickly became famous, and many people took it as proof that intelligent life once existed on the planet. The evidence gathered since then, however, points clearly in the other direction.

The strongest evidence comes from better photographs. The original Viking image was blurry, and the "features" of the face were mostly shadows created by the angle of the sun. In 1998, the Mars Global Surveyor flew over Cydonia and took a picture ten times sharper than the original. The face was gone. What the camera showed instead was an ordinary hill with slopes and ridges. Some people objected that clouds had been in the way, so NASA photographed the site again in 2001, on a clear summer day, using the camera's maximum resolution. Each pixel in that image covered only 1.56 meters, compared with 43 meters in 1976. At that level of detail, objects as small as airplanes or small shacks would have been visible if they existed. Nothing of the kind appeared.
