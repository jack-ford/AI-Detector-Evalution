What people call the "Face on Mars" is simply a natural landform, not something an alien civilization left behind. In 1976 the Viking 1 spacecraft took pictures of the Cydonia region, and one showed a mesa with a startling resemblance to a human face: eyes, nose and mouth all included. The photo soon became famous, and many took it as proof that intelligent beings once lived on the planet. Evidence collected since then points firmly the other way.

Better photographs make the strongest case. Viking's original image was fuzzy, and most of the face's "features" were shadows produced by the angle of the sun. When the Mars Global Surveyor passed over Cydonia in 1998, it took a picture ten times sharper, and the face had vanished. The camera revealed an ordinary hill with slopes and ridges. Some people complained that clouds had blocked the view, so in 2001 NASA photographed the site once more, on a clear summer day and at the camera's highest resolution. A pixel in that image spanned just 1.56 meters, against 43 meters in 1976. At such detail, anything as small as an airplane or a little shack would have shown up if it were there. Nothing of the sort appeared.
