For most of history, going to school meant being in a particular building at a particular time. Online and video classes have changed that, and many schools now have to decide whether to let students attend from home. I think they should offer the option. Distance learning is not right for every student, but for those who need it, it can make the difference between keeping up and falling behind.

The clearest benefit is for students who cannot easily get to school. A student recovering from surgery, caring for a younger sibling, or living an hour's bus ride from campus loses instruction every time something goes wrong. With a video class, that student can still hear the lesson, ask questions, and turn in work on time. Missing a week of algebra in person can take a month to make up. Missing no days at all is obviously better.

Learning from home also lets students work at a pace that suits them. In a classroom of thirty, the teacher has to move at one speed. Some students are bored, and others are lost but too embarrassed to say so. Recorded lessons can be paused and replayed. A student who did not understand how to balance a chemical equation the first time can watch the explanation again without holding anyone else up.

Critics point out that students at home are more easily distracted and miss out on time with their classmates. Both concerns are real. A phone is a stronger temptation in a bedroom than in a classroom, and friendships are harder to build through a screen. However, these are reasons to design online classes carefully, with live discussions and regular check-ins, and not reasons to refuse them.
