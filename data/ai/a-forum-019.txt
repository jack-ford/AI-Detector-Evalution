So from what I know, the good job market for math people was mostly in the 1950s and 60s. Back then alot of new colleges were being built and the old ones were getting way bigger, because of the GI Bill and then later all the baby boomers started going to college. Also the government got really scared after Sputnik and started putting a ton of money into math and science. So basically there was more jobs then there were people with PhDs to fill them. I read that some people got hired as professors before they even finished their thesis, which sounds crazy now.

The thing is, that wasn't really normal. It only happened because the whole system was growing super fast all at once. A professor trains a bunch of PhD students in their career, maybe ten or more, but when they retire there is only one job that opens up, which is theirs. So unless the number of jobs keeps growing every year, most of those students aren't going to get a job like their advisor has. In the 60s the number did keep growing so it worked out. Then in the 70s it stopped growing and things got bad pretty quick.

Could it come back? Honestly I dont think so, at least not in the US. You would need colleges to start expanding like crazy again and right now it's kind of the opposite, a lot of them are cutting stuff.
