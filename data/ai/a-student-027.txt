Last June my English teacher handed us a list of four novels and a two-page sheet of questions for each. I groaned along with everyone else. By August I had read two of them on the bus to my job at the pool, and when school started I already knew who Atticus Finch was while the kids from the other class, who got to "design their own reading project," were still arguing over whether a movie counted. That is why I think summer projects should be made by teachers. A teacher knows what September holds. Mine knew we would open with a unit on the trial scenes, so her questions pointed straight at them. Students can't know that. We also aren't great at pushing ourselves in July; given the choice, I would have picked the shortest book in the library and written about it the night before. Some people say choosing your own project makes you care more. Maybe. It didn't make my cousin care about his poster on sharks.
