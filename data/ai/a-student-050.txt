Through most of history, school meant being in a certain building at a certain hour. Online and video classes have altered that, and many schools must now choose whether students may attend from home. I believe schools should offer it. Distance learning doesn't suit every student, yet for those who need it, it can decide whether they keep up or fall behind.

Students who have trouble getting to school gain the most. Someone recovering from surgery, looking after a younger sibling, or living an hour's bus ride from campus loses lessons every time something goes wrong. A video class lets that student still hear the lesson, ask questions, and hand in work on time. A week of missed algebra can take a month to make up in person, and missing no days at all is clearly better.

Studying at home also lets students set their own pace. In a room of thirty, a teacher can only go at one speed. Some students are bored while others are lost and too embarrassed to admit it. Recorded lessons can be paused and played again. A student who didn't follow how to balance a chemical equation the first time can rewatch the explanation without holding anyone up.

Critics say students at home get distracted more easily and lose time with classmates. Both worries are real. A phone tempts more in a bedroom than in a classroom, and it is harder to make friends through a screen. Still, those are reasons to design online classes with care, using live discussions and regular check-ins, and not reasons to turn them down.
