It's very hard to save "too much" for retirement in any absolute sense, but it is possible to have too much of your savings locked in the wrong place, and I suspect that's the real question here.

A common rule of thumb is to have around three times your salary saved by 40, six times by 50, and roughly ten times by the time you retire in your mid-sixties. If you're well ahead of that, you aren't doing anything wrong. You've bought yourself options: retiring earlier, taking a lower-paying job you'd enjoy more, or easing off contributions for a few years.

What I would check is balance. If nearly everything is in pre-tax accounts like a traditional 401(k), you generally can't touch it before 59½ without a penalty, and every dollar will be taxed as ordinary income when it comes out. Before adding more there, make sure you have an emergency fund covering several months of expenses, money in ordinary taxable accounts for goals that come before retirement (a house, a child's education), and some Roth savings so you can manage your tax bracket later.

If those are in place and you're comfortably meeting your current expenses, keep going. If retirement contributions are forcing you to carry credit card debt or skip things that matter to you now, scale back to the employer match and redirect the rest.
