"She'll fly," Jim said, wiping his hands on a rag that was mostly grease already. "The strut's bent about an inch. I wired it. Don't look at me like that, Tuck, I've seen worse wired."

"On a kite, maybe."

Sergeant Mackay had come down to the float with a tin mug of tea in each fist. He gave one to the trapper, kept the other, and squinted at the Fairchild as if it were a horse somebody wanted him to buy. "How far to the cache, did you say?"

"Forty miles. Fifty if the lake's still froze at the north end."

"Then two of you go and one stays with Pierre." The sergeant drank. "He knows who shot at you. He'll say so by supper if nobody crowds him."

Tuck opened his mouth and shut it again. He had flown eleven hundred miles to see that cache, had slept two nights under a wing with the mosquitoes, and now he was to sit on an upturned canoe and watch an old Frenchman whittle.

"I'll stay," he said. It came out sour.

Mackay nodded and went on looking at the strut.
