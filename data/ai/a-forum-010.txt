The splatter comes from water, not from the oil itself. Any moisture on the surface of the steak flashes into steam the instant it touches fat that is well above 100°C, and the expanding steam throws droplets of oil out of the pan.

That means the fix is mostly about preparation. Pat the steak thoroughly dry with paper towels right before it goes in, and if you salt it, either do so immediately beforehand or at least 40 minutes ahead so the drawn-out moisture has time to be reabsorbed. Use only a thin film of oil; a deep pool gives you more to spray around. A splatter screen over the pan catches most of what's left without trapping steam the way a lid would.
