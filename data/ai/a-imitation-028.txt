Summer is often viewed as a time when learning comes to a halt. To prevent this, many schools assign projects for students to complete over the break. While some argue that students should design these projects themselves, the projects should be designed by teachers. Teachers understand what students will need to know for the upcoming year, and a teacher-designed project ensures that every student is held to the same standard.

Teachers have spent years studying their subject and planning their curriculum. Because of this, they know exactly which skills students will need when they return in the fall. For instance, a chemistry teacher may assign a project that reviews the algebra needed to balance equations, since this is where many students struggle in the first month. A student designing their own project would have no way of knowing this. They would most likely choose a topic that they already feel comfortable with, which defeats the purpose of preparing for a new class.

Additionally, a project designed by the teacher creates fairness among students. If every student creates their own assignment, the level of difficulty will vary greatly. One student might write a ten-page research paper while another builds a simple poster. Grading these projects against one another would be nearly impossible for a teacher to do fairly.
