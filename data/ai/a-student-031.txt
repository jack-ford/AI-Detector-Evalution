My cousin Derek rear-ended a landscaping trailer on Route 9 the summer he turned seventeen. He was doing maybe thirty. He'd looked down to read a text from his girlfriend, four words long, and when he looked up the trailer's brake lights were already filling the windshield. Nobody died. He broke his wrist on the steering wheel. His mom's Corolla went to the scrapyard with a rake handle through the radiator.

I think about that rake handle a lot.

Derek isn't stupid, and he wasn't a reckless driver. He'd had his license for five months and had never gotten a ticket. He just believed what most people believe, that a glance at a phone takes no time at all. It takes about five seconds to read a text. At 55 miles an hour a car covers the length of a football field in five seconds, and for that whole stretch the driver might as well have a paper bag over his head.

So should drivers be allowed to use phones at all while the car is moving? No. Not to text, and not to hold one against an ear and argue with somebody about dinner.

People will say hands-free calls are different because your eyes stay on the road. Eyes aren't the whole problem, though. Try doing long division out loud while somebody tells you a story, and see how much of the story you remember. A driver on a call is looking straight at the stop sign and still rolling through it, because the part of his brain that should be noticing stop signs is busy with the conversation. My driver's ed teacher, Mr. Kowalczyk, called this "looking without seeing," and he made us watch a video where a woman on a headset drove past a guy in a gorilla suit and never noticed him.
