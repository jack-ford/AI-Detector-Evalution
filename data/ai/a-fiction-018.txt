He has been before the magistrates eleven times, and he speaks of the bench with the tolerant affection a man keeps for a landlord who has never quite managed to evict him. A month in gaol he regards as a rest. I asked him once whether he had never thought of honest work, and he looked at me as though I had proposed that he should cut off a hand. What draws him is not the few shillings a stolen terrier fetches in Shoreditch. It is the stalking of the animal, the patience, the bit of liver in the pocket, the moment when a dog worth ten pounds trots after him of its own free will.

Last Tuesday he walked with me as far as the canal. A lady's spaniel was sunning itself on a doorstep, and I felt him slow beside me. "Don't look at it," he said under his breath. "They always know when you look.
