When my bike chain snapped in July, I asked my uncle what to do and he said to buy a whole new bike. Two hundred dollars. I almost did it. Then the guy at the hardware store told me a chain costs eleven bucks, and my neighbor, who races on weekends, showed me how to put it on in about ten minutes.

That is why I ask more than one person before I decide something. My uncle wasn't lying to me. He just doesn't fix things, he replaces them, so that's the answer he had. Every person you ask is answering from their own life. One person gives you one life's worth of advice, and it might be the wrong life for your problem.

It also matters who you ask second.
