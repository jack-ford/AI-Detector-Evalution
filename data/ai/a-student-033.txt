Our school is thinking about making every student do community service, and I'm against it, even though I already do some. On Saturdays I help at the food pantry behind St. Luke's. I stack cans, break down boxes, and carry bags out to cars for people who can't manage them. Nobody made me. My neighbor Mrs. Okafor runs the place and asked if I had a free morning, and one morning turned into most of a year.

Here is what I've noticed. The kids who show up because a judge or a coach sent them are easy to spot. They stand by the door checking their phones. One boy spent two hours "sorting" a single shelf of soup. Mrs. Okafor has to find jobs for them and then check the jobs afterward, which is more work for her than if they had stayed home. If the school sends four hundred students out with a form to get signed, a lot of places are going to get that boy times four hundred.

There is also the time. I can give up a Saturday morning because I don't play a fall sport and my parents drive me. My friend Dante watches his two little sisters every day until his mom gets home at seven. He is already helping somebody. Telling him he owes thirty more hours to strangers, or he doesn't graduate, seems backwards to me.

I'd rather the school just made it easy. Put a list on the wall outside the office with names and phone numbers. Let a club count it. Give people a ride. Some will never sign up. The pantry will survive without them; it mostly needs people on the third Saturday, when the truck comes.
