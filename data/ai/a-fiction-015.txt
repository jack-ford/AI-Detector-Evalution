He had told himself, coming up in the cab, that he would watch her tonight as a stranger might, coolly, and so learn the truth. It was a resolve that lasted until she turned her head. Whatever Hartley had been to her last summer, there was nothing of him in the look she gave Dering across the little table; it was frank, a trifle amused, and wholly his. She had laughed at Hartley's story with the rest, neither more nor less than civility asked, and had gone back at once to the business of drawing off her gloves. Would a woman who cared have managed that so easily? Dering thought not. He knew her for a creature of quick angers and quicker forgivenesses, incapable of hiding a hurt for five minutes together, and he loved her the better for it; a saint would have wearied him in a week. The shaded candles laid a soft gold on her bare shoulders and woke copper lights in the dark coils of her hair, and under their level brows her grey eyes rested on him with a friendliness that set his pulse hammering and made him, for the moment, absurdly sure of his future.
