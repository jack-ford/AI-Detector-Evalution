There are two separate problems here, and it helps to pull them apart, because one is about your feelings and the other is about a contract.

On the demotion: reorganizations reshuffle titles all the time, and it usually says very little about the person who lost one. After a merger there are two team leads and one team, and somebody has to step down. Six months of tenure is a thin argument against a counterpart who has been around for years, however good those six months were. I'd treat it as a setback in title rather than a verdict on your work, and I'd avoid saying anything to your manager right now that you can't take back.

On the repayment clause: get the actual document out and read it before you decide anything. These agreements (relocation, training, sign-on bonus) are normally prorated, so the amount you owe shrinks every month you stay. Find out the exact figure today and the date it drops to zero. Also check what the clause says about changes to your role. Some agreements are tied to the specific position you were hired into, and in some jurisdictions a significant unilateral change to your job weakens the employer's ability to enforce repayment. That's a question for an employment lawyer, and an hour of their time costs far less than the sum you're worried about.
