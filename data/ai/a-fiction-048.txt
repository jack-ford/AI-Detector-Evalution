"But that isn't the question. He never took the money, half the form is aware of it, and yet no one will share a table with him. That strikes me as cowardly."

"Cowardly isn't quite the word, Margaret. Fellows follow the crowd, that's all."

"Then someone ought to quit following." Laying aside the racket she had been restringing, she faced him directly. "You captain the eleven, Ned. Walk across the dining hall tonight and take the seat next to Lawrence, and before the soup has cooled six others will be sitting there too."

"To hear you tell it, nothing could be easier."

"It is easy. It's merely uncomfortable, which is something else entirely."

Bradley gave a short laugh and scraped at the turf with his heel. "And if I do, and Tom Wheelock's set cuts me as well?"

"Then you'll know what they're worth. Will you do it?"

"I'll give it some thought."

"That means yes," said Margaret, and took up her racket again.

Back at the dormitory he repeated the whole conversation to Hollis.

"Well, she's right about Wheelock, at any rate," Hollis said.
