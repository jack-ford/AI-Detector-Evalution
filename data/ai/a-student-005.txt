I am writing to support the idea of requiring every student to complete some community service before graduating. I know many of my classmates will object that their schedules are already full, and that is a fair concern. Still, I think the benefits outweigh the cost in time.

First, service connects students to the place where they live. Most of us travel between home, school and practice without ever noticing who keeps the park clean or who stocks the food pantry on Elm Street. Spending a few Saturdays doing that work ourselves changes how we see our town. Last spring I helped sort donations at the pantry for one afternoon, and I was surprised to recognize two families from my own neighborhood in the line.

Second, service teaches skills that classes do not. Showing up on time for people who are counting on you, taking directions from an adult who is not a teacher, and finishing a dull task properly are all things employers expect.

A requirement does not have to be heavy. Ten hours a semester, with a list of approved places and the freedom to choose among them, would be enough to get students started without hurting their grades or their sports. Many would keep going on their own.

I hope you will consider making service part of what it means to graduate from our school.
