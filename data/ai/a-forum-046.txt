Two different problems are tangled together here, and you'll think more clearly if you separate them, since one concerns how you feel and the other concerns a contract.

Start with the demotion. Titles get reshuffled in reorganizations constantly, and what happens to one person's title rarely says much about that person. A merger leaves two team leads for one team, so somebody has to give way. Six months on the job, however good they were, is a weak case against a counterpart with years behind them. I'd see this as a setback in your title and not a judgment of your work, and for now I wouldn't say anything to your manager that you couldn't later take back.

Then there's the repayment clause. Before deciding anything, dig out the document and read it. Agreements of this kind (relocation, training, sign-on bonus) are usually prorated, so every month you stay lowers what you'd owe. Work out the exact figure today, and the date it reaches zero. Look, too, at what the clause says about changes to your role. Some are tied to the particular position you were hired for, and in some places a major change to your job imposed by the employer weakens their ability to demand repayment. An employment lawyer is the person to ask, and an hour of their time costs a lot less than the sum you're worried about.
