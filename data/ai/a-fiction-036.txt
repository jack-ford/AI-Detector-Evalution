so he kept going down the hall, what else was he gonna do, stand there all night. the candle was basically useless it lit up his own hand and about two feet of wallpaper and thats it, everything past that was just black, and the hallway felt way longer than it ever did when he was a kid which made no sense because he was bigger now. shouldve felt smaller right. it didnt.

he tried telling himself it was a fine old house, good bones, his grandfathers house, people would pay real money for a place like this. didnt help much. honestly the only thing that made him feel better was thinking about selling it, signing the papers and never walking in here again, let somebody else have the creaks and the cold spots and whatever else

every door on the left side was open. he didnt look in. he knew what was in there anyway, beds with sheets over them, a wardrobe, the washstand with the cracked jug, but knowing it and turning his head to check were two diffrent things and he wasnt going to turn his head. Not tonight. he just counted them as he went past, one, two, three, like a kid counting stairs.

then about halfway the floor changed under his feet, carpet to bare boards, and he remembred this was where the old runner ended, the one with the roses, his mother always said they'd replace it and never did. and weirdly that was the thing that calmed him down. something so stupid and ordinary. he stopped and breathed for a second and his heart quit banging so hard

but he also knew, standing there, that if hed looked back at the landing a minute ago when he heard it he wouldve seen something. he was sure of that now. and he hadnt looked, and it hadn't followed, and the back stairs were maybe twenty feet off.
