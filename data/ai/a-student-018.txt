When Luke Bomberger's friend Don Reist invited him to work on a cattle boat bound for Europe in 1945, Luke had just graduated from high school and was working two part-time jobs, one in a grocery store and one in a bank. He had never traveled far from his small town. By the time he finished his service two years later, he had crossed the Atlantic sixteen times and the Pacific twice. His story shows why other young people should consider joining the Seagoing Cowboys program: it offers the chance to help people in need while seeing parts of the world most of us only read about.

The first reason to join is that the work matters. After World War II, many countries were left in ruins, and farms had lost the horses, cows, and mules they depended on. The Seagoing Cowboys cared for these animals on the long voyage overseas so that families could plow their fields and feed themselves again. Luke fed and watered the animals two or three times a day and cleaned their stalls. It was hard, tiring work, and on one trip he cracked his ribs after slipping on a wet ladder during a storm. Still, he said the experience made him more aware of people in other countries and what they needed.
