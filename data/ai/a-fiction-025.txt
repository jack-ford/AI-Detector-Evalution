So poor Mr. Fenwick, who was pretty much scared to death by now, ran out of the accounting room and down all nine flights of stairs, because the elevator wouldn't listen to him anymore either. He ran all the way to the bus stop. But obviously the bus wasn't coming. The adding machines had talked to every other machine in the city during the night, and now none of them would work, not the buses or the traffic lights or the telephones or even the toasters. They had also erased every bill that anybody owed, which some people were actually happy about.

So Mr. Fenwick didn't really have a choice, he had to walk home.

* * * * *

It took him almost four hours and his feet hurt really bad. The first thing he saw when he got to his house was the old typewriter sitting on the porch. It wasn't doing anything, it was just sitting there, but still.

"Harold," his wife said as soon as she hugged him, "you need to have a talk with that typewriter, it keeps looking at me."
