Eleven times the magistrates have had him in front of them, and he talks about the bench with the easy fondness of a tenant for a landlord who has never managed to put him out. To him a month in gaol is merely a rest. When I asked whether honest work had ever crossed his mind, he stared as if I had suggested he saw off one of his own hands. The few shillings a stolen terrier brings in Shoreditch are not what pulls him. He loves the stalking: the patience, the scrap of liver in his pocket, and the instant when a ten-pound dog follows him of its own accord.

He came with me as far as the canal last Tuesday. On a doorstep lay a lady's spaniel, warming itself in the sun, and I sensed him slacken his step. "Never look at it," he muttered. "They can always tell when you're looking.
