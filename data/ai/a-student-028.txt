Last fall our starting point guard, Darnell, sat out six games because he had a 68 in Algebra II. The policy at my school says you need a C in every class to play. The principal now wants to raise that to a B average, and I think that would wreck more than it fixes.

Start with who actually gets cut. I asked Coach Reyes how many of his varsity players have a B average right now. Seven out of fifteen. So eight kids lose basketball in November, and those eight are not going to walk straight to the library at 3:15. Some of them come to school because of practice. Darnell told me that himself. When he was benched he missed four days of school in three weeks, and he hadn't missed four all of the year before.

A C is already hard for some people. My cousin works at her mom's nail salon until nine on weeknights and still pulls C's and one B, in art. She plays the clarinet in marching band. Under the new rule she'd be out, and band is the only reason she has friends outside her own street.

People who like the B rule say sports are a privilege. Fine. But a 79 and an 80 are one quiz question apart, and I don't see why one question should decide whether a kid gets to run track. Teachers grade differently too. Mr. Havlik gives almost nobody an A.

What worked for Darnell was study table. Coach made him sit in room 114 for forty minutes before practice with a senior who had already passed the class. He got his grade up to a 74 by Christmas.
