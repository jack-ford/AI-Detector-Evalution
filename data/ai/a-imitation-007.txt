Venus is called the "Evening Star" but it is actualy a planet, and one of the most dangerous ones in our solar system. In "The Challenge of Exploring Venus" the author suggests that studying Venus is a worthy pursuit despite the dangers it presents, and he supports this idea well by giving solutions to the problems instead of just ignoring them.

The author supports his claim by showing that scientists already have ideas for how to survive the conditions. He admits that the planet is extremly hostile when he says "temperatures average over 800 degrees Fahrenheit, and the atmospheric pressure is 90 times greater than what we experience on our own planet" (3). This is important because it shows the author is not hiding the dangers from reader. Then he explains NASA's idea of a "blimp-like vehicle hovering 30 or so miles above the roiling Venusian landscape" (5). Up that high the tempature would be around 170 degrees and the pressure would be close to sea level on Earth. That is still hot but humans could survive it, so the author proves that the danger is something we can work around and not a reason to give up on Venus completly.
