The Electoral College should be replaced with a national popular vote. Under the current system, a candidate can win the presidency while receiving fewer votes than an opponent, which happened in 2000 when Al Gore won the popular vote but lost the election. This outcome is hard to defend in a democracy. The winner-take-all rule used by most states also means candidates spend nearly all of their time in a handful of swing states such as Ohio and Florida, while voters in states like California and Texas are largely ignored. Supporters argue that the Electoral College produces a clear winner and forces candidates to appeal to different regions, but those benefits do not outweigh the basic unfairness of unequal votes.
