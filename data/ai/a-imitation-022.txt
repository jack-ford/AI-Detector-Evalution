Picture the worst vacation spot you can think of. Now make it 800 degrees, add clouds made of acid, and crank the air pressure up until it could flatten a submarine. Congratulations, you have just pictured Venus. So why on Earth (pun intended) would anybody want to go there? According to the author of "The Challenge of Exploring Venus," the answer is simple. Because it's worth it.

I'll admit, when I first read the article I thought the author was a little crazy. Who looks at a planet that melts metal and says, 'yeah, let's send Greg'? But the more I read, the more it started to make sense. The author doesn't pretend Venus is safe. He actually spends like half the article listing all the ways it could kill you, which is honestly a weird way to convince somebody. But that's kind of what makes his argument good. He isn't hiding anything from us. He lays out the dangers first, and then shows that NASA already has a plan for them.

That plan is to not land at all. Instead, astronauts would float about thirty miles above the surface in a vehicle sort of like a blimp. Up there, the temperature is around 170 degrees, which is toasty, but people in Arizona basically deal with that every summer. The pressure is close to what we have at sea level too.
