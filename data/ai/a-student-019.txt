In the article "Making Mona Lisa Smile" the author talks about a new technology called the Facial Action Coding System that can read peoples emotions by looking at their face. Some people think this would be good to use in classrooms and some people don't. In my opinion I think that it would not be that valuable in a classroom, because students deserve privacy and also because a computer can't really know what a person is feeling.

First of all, I think it's kind of an invasion of privacy. If I am sitting in class and I'm having a bad day, I don't really want a computer telling my teacher about it. Maybe something happened at home or I got in a fight with my friend. That has nothing to do with the lesson. The article says the computer can tell if your happy, sad, angry, and other emotions, but alot of students would not be comfortable with a camera watching there face the whole class period. I know I wouldn't. It would make me nervous and then I would probably pay attention even less.

Another reason is that the computer could be wrong. In the article it says the software looked at the Mona Lisa and said she was 83 percent happy and 9 percent disgusted. But how do they even know that's right, nobody can ask her. People make faces for all kinds of reasons. Sometimes I look bored when I'm actually thinking really hard about something, and my mom always says I look mad when I'm not. If the computer thinks I'm confused and changes the lesson to be easier, but I wasn't actually confused, then that just wastes time for me.

The author does make a good point that a computer could help when a student is confused and the teacher doesn't notice. I get that. But a teacher can just ask the class if they understand, or walk around and check on people, which is what most of my teachers do anyways. That works fine and it doesn't cost the school a bunch of money for new computers and cameras.
