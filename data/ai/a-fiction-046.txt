On the way up in the cab he had promised himself to study her that evening like a stranger, with a cool eye, and thereby find out the truth. The promise held only until she turned her head. Whatever Hartley might have meant to her the summer before, the glance she gave Dering over the small table had no trace of him in it. It was open and slightly amused, and entirely Dering's. She had laughed at Hartley's story along with everyone else, no more and no less than politeness required, then turned straight back to the business of easing off her gloves. Could a woman in love have done that so easily? Dering decided she could not. He knew her as quick to anger and quicker to forgive, unable to conceal a hurt for even five minutes, and he loved her all the more for it, since a saint would have tired him within a week. Soft gold from the shaded candles lay across her bare shoulders and brought out copper in the dark coils of her hair, while beneath level brows her grey eyes met his with a friendliness that made his pulse pound and left him, for the moment, ridiculously certain of his future.
